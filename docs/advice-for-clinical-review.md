# HyperSync: advice and thresholds for clinical review

_Generated by `python -m scripts.export_advice`; do not edit by hand. To change wording or a cut-off, edit the code (file names are given below), regenerate, and have this sheet re-signed._

Everything here is general lifestyle guidance shown to patients. None of it is a diagnosis or a prescription.

## 1. Blood pressure bands (mmHg)

Source: `backend/app/core/bp_classification.py` (mirrored in `frontend/src/utils/bpStatus.js`).

| Band | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Low | systolic below 90 **or** diastolic below 60 |
| High | systolic 150 or above **or** diastolic 90 or above |
| Elevated | systolic 135 or above **or** diastolic 85 or above |
| Controlled | otherwise |

## 2. Habits patients log, and what is said about them

Source: `backend/app/core/factor_insights.py`, `lifestyle_factors.py`.

### Salt

- **Treated as a risk when:** Moderate or high
- **Patient is told:** Your logged salt intake has been high. Cutting back on added salt and salty processed foods is one of the most effective lifestyle changes for lowering blood pressure.
- **Advice to avoid it:** Try seasoning with herbs, lemon or spices instead of salt, and go easy on stock cubes, sauces, processed meat and salted snacks.
- **Used in comparisons as:** "ate a moderate or high amount of salt" versus "ate little salt"

### Alcohol

- **Treated as a risk when:** Any alcohol
- **Patient is told:** You've logged alcohol consumption recently. Drinking less alcohol helps lower blood pressure.
- **Advice to avoid it:** Keep some days completely alcohol-free, and keep to a small amount on the days you do drink.
- **Used in comparisons as:** "drank alcohol" versus "didn't drink alcohol"

### Smoking

- **Treated as a risk when:** Smoked
- **Patient is told:** You've logged smoking recently. Smoking raises cardiovascular risk and temporarily raises blood pressure after each cigarette. Consider support resources for quitting.
- **Advice to avoid it:** Ask your clinic about help to quit. Cutting down is a good start, and every smoke-free day counts.
- **Used in comparisons as:** "smoked" versus "didn't smoke"

### Physical activity

- **Treated as a risk when:** Under 20 minutes
- **Patient is told:** Your logged physical activity has been low. Regular moderate activity, such as brisk walking on most days, helps lower blood pressure.
- **Advice to avoid it:** Start with a 10-minute brisk walk after a meal and build towards about 30 minutes on most days.
- **Used in comparisons as:** "were active for under 20 minutes" versus "were more active"

### Diet followed

- **Treated as a risk when:** Did not follow the advised diet
- **Patient is told:** You've logged not meeting the recommended fruit and vegetable intake. Eating more fruit and vegetables helps lower blood pressure.
- **Advice to avoid it:** Add a fruit or vegetables to every meal, and swap fried or fatty food for boiled, grilled or steamed.
- **Used in comparisons as:** "didn't eat as advised" versus "ate as advised"

### Sleep

- **Treated as a risk when:** Under 6 hours
- **Patient is told:** You've logged under 6 hours of sleep. Poor sleep is linked to higher blood pressure.
- **Advice to avoid it:** Aim for 7 to 9 hours: keep a regular bedtime, put screens away 30 minutes before bed, and avoid coffee or tea late in the day.
- **Used in comparisons as:** "slept under 6 hours" versus "slept 6 hours or more"

### Stress

- **Treated as a risk when:** High
- **Patient is told:** You've logged high stress. Stress can raise blood pressure.
- **Advice to avoid it:** Try five minutes of slow breathing (in for 4, out for 6), a short walk, or talking things through with someone you trust.
- **Used in comparisons as:** "logged high stress" versus "logged lower stress"

### Coffee or tea

- **Treated as a risk when:** High
- **Patient is told:** You've logged a lot of coffee or tea. Caffeine can push blood pressure up for a few hours after each cup.
- **Advice to avoid it:** Keep to one or two cups a day, have none after mid-afternoon, and wait 30 minutes after a cup before measuring your blood pressure.
- **Used in comparisons as:** "had a lot of coffee or tea" versus "had less coffee or tea"

### Medication

- **Treated as a risk when:** Missed a dose
- **Patient is told:** You've logged a missed medication dose. Missing doses can lead to higher readings.
- **Advice to avoid it:** Take it at the same time each day, set a phone reminder, never double up after a missed dose, and tell your clinician if you keep missing doses.
- **Used in comparisons as:** "missed your medicine" versus "took your medicine"

### Temperature

- **Treated as a risk when:** Cold
- **Patient is told:** You've logged cold weather. Cold narrows blood vessels and can push blood pressure up.
- **Advice to avoid it:** Dress in warm layers (head, hands and feet especially), warm up indoors before measuring, and avoid stepping straight from a warm bed into the cold.
- **Used in comparisons as:** "logged cold weather" versus "logged mild or hot weather"

### Noise

- **Treated as a risk when:** Loud
- **Patient is told:** You've logged a loud day. Ongoing loud noise is a stressor and is linked to higher blood pressure.
- **Advice to avoid it:** Use ear protection or noise-cancelling earphones where you can, take quiet breaks away from the noise, and measure your blood pressure in a quiet room.
- **Used in comparisons as:** "had a loud day" versus "had a quiet or moderate day"

## 3. How each reading was taken

Patients may record whether they rested for about 5 minutes first, and whether they had coffee/tea, smoked or exercised in the previous 30 minutes.

- Raised when **2 or more** readings in the period were taken without rest or soon after a stimulant.
- When at least 2 properly taken readings exist to compare, the gap is shown if it is 5 mmHg systolic or more.
- **Advice:** Sit quietly with your back supported and feet flat for 5 minutes, and wait 30 minutes after coffee, tea, smoking or exercise before you measure. If your readings are only high when taken in a hurry, tell your clinician.

## 4. When the system links a habit to the patient's own readings

- A habit is raised when risky on the most recent entry, or on **2+ days** in the period.
- A personal pattern needs **2+ days on each side** (risky and not risky) with a reading, and an average systolic gap of **5 mmHg or more**.
- Wording always says it is a link in the patient's own readings, not proof of cause.
- Habits that occurred on exactly the same days are reported together.
- At most 2 pattern findings are shown. Smoking and missed medication are never the habits left out for space; anything cut is named.
